# Problem Statement

## Affected Audience

**Primary:** Supply-chain control-tower operators and logistics incident managers who must respond to active disruptions (port closures, severe weather, strikes, geopolitical restrictions) within minutes.

**Secondary:** Fleet managers, cold-chain compliance officers, carrier operations managers, and warehouse/distribution-center managers who depend on real-time visibility into shipment status and asset availability.

## Why Existing Solutions Don't Solve It

1. **Reactive dashboards:** Most supply-chain visibility platforms show historical data and current status but do not automatically identify which shipments are affected by a specific disruption or quantify the impact.

2. **Manual rerouting:** Logistics teams manually search for alternative routes and carriers, a process that consumes 30–60 minutes per disruption event. By then, cargo value and customer commitments are already at risk.

3. **Siloed cold-chain monitoring:** Temperature sensors generate alerts only after excursions occur. By the time an alert reaches operations, the product may already be damaged. Excursion severity is often unclear without manual policy review.

4. **No asset visibility:** Idle fleet assets (trucks, containers, reefer units) are not automatically matched to disrupted shipments. Redeployment decisions are made manually or not at all, leaving capacity unused while cargo is rerouted at higher cost.

5. **Unexplainable AI:** Existing AI-driven supply-chain tools generate recommendations without exposing the underlying logic, making it impossible for operators to trust or audit decisions in high-stakes scenarios.

## Quantified Pain (Where Data Is Available)

- **Decision latency:** Manual disruption response takes 30–90 minutes; each hour of delay increases cargo loss exposure and customer SLA breach risk.
- **Cargo value at risk:** A single port disruption can affect 100–500 shipments; average cargo value per shipment ranges from $50,000 to $500,000+. A 2-hour delay in rerouting decisions exposes $5M–$50M+ in cargo value.
- **Cold-chain damage:** Temperature excursions during transit result in product loss, regulatory fines, and customer recalls. Early detection can reduce loss by 40–60% if rerouting or isolation decisions are made before delivery.
- **Asset utilization:** Fleet idle rates of 15–25% are common during disruptions because asset-to-shipment matching is manual. Automated redeployment can reduce idle rates by 30–40%.

## Why This Matters Now

1. **Disruption frequency is increasing:** Climate change, geopolitical instability, and supply-chain fragmentation have increased the frequency and severity of disruptions. Operations teams need faster response mechanisms.

2. **Regulatory pressure on cold-chain:** FDA, CDC, and international regulators are tightening cold-chain compliance requirements. Automated excursion detection and evidence trails are becoming competitive advantages.

3. **Customer expectations:** E-commerce and just-in-time logistics have compressed delivery windows. A single missed SLA can result in customer churn and regulatory penalties.

4. **AI readiness:** Large language models and optimization engines are now mature enough to support real-time decision support, but only if they are constrained by deterministic logic and explainability requirements.

5. **Hackathon theme alignment:** "Turn idea into impact faster" directly maps to reducing decision latency from 60 minutes to under 60 seconds, converting raw disruption signals into verified operational actions.
